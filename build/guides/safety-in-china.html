Is China Safe for Tourists? Practical Safety Guide 2026

Short answer: yes, China is one of the safest countries for tourists. Violent crime against foreigners is extremely rare. The bigger risks are petty scams, traffic, and getting lost in a language you don't speak. Here's the practical picture.

The good news

  • Low violent crime: street crime, muggings, and pickpocketing are far rarer than in most Western cities.
  • Safe at night: most cities feel safe walking at night, even alone.
  • Helpful locals: if you look lost, someone will usually try to help — even with no shared language.
  • Police presence: visible in tourist areas and transport hubs.

What to actually watch for

Common tourist scams

  1. Tea house / art gallery scam: a friendly local invites you for tea or to see an art exhibition, then presents a huge bill. Politely decline invitations from strangers near tourist sites.
  2. Taxi overcharging: insist on the meter, or use Didi (ride-hailing app) which shows the price upfront.
  3. "Temple closed" trick: someone tells you a temple is closed and offers to take you to a "better" one (which is a shop). Ignore and walk on.
  4. Currency exchange on the street: always use banks or ATMs.
  5. Fake "police" or "officials": real police never ask tourists for money. If approached, walk to a well-lit public area.

Practical safety tips

  • Traffic is the real danger — Chinese cities have heavy traffic and scooters everywhere. Look both ways twice, and use pedestrian crossings.
  • Keep your passport safe — carry a photocopy or photo; leave the original in the hotel safe.
  • Watch your phone — snatch-and-grab from scooters happens in busy areas. Don't hold your phone near the road.
  • Drink responsibly — baijiu (Chinese liquor) is strong; pace yourself.
  • Air quality — check the AQI in winter in northern cities; bring a mask if you're sensitive.

Emergency numbers

Emergency Number
Police 110
Fire 119
Ambulance 120
Tourist complaint hotline 12301
Foreign affairs (consular help) Contact your embassy

Save these in your phone before you arrive. Your embassy's 24-hour line is the best resource for lost passports or legal trouble.

Health & medical

  • Hospitals: major cities have international hospitals with English-speaking staff (e.g., Beijing United Family, Shanghai East International).
  • Pharmacies: 药店 (yàodiàn) sell common medicines over the counter. Bring your own prescription meds with a doctor's note.
  • Tap water: don't drink it. Bottled water is cheap everywhere.
  • Travel insurance: strongly recommended — medical costs for foreigners can be high, and insurance covers evacuation.

FAQ

Q: Is China safe for solo female travelers? A: Generally yes — China is considered one of the safer destinations for solo women. Standard precautions apply, especially at night.

Q: Are there areas to avoid? A: No major no-go zones for tourists. Use common sense in crowded markets and at night.

Q: What if I lose my passport? A: Report it to the police (get a report), then contact your embassy for an emergency travel document.

Q: Do I need travel insurance? A: Yes — medical costs and trip disruptions are the biggest financial risks. Get comprehensive coverage.

Q: Is it safe to use Alipay/WeChat Pay? A: Yes — they're the standard payment methods. Just use the official apps and enable fingerprint/Face ID.

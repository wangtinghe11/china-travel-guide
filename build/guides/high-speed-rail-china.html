China High-Speed Rail: How to Book Tickets & Ride the Trains

China's high-speed rail network is the largest in the world — over 40,000 km of track. Trains are fast (up to 350 km/h), frequent, clean, and surprisingly easy for foreigners. This guide covers booking, boarding, and riding.

Why take the train?

  • Speed: Beijing–Shanghai in 4.5 hours; Shanghai–Hangzhou in 45 minutes; Guangzhou–Shenzhen in 30 minutes.
  • Punctuality: trains run on time, almost to the second.
  • Comfort: wide seats, power outlets, free WiFi on many routes, and a dining car.
  • Location: stations are usually closer to city centers than airports.

How to book tickets

Option 1: Official 12306 app (cheapest)

  1. Download 12306 (official app, has an English version) or use the website 12306.cn.
  2. Register with your passport number — this is your ticket ID.
  3. Search your route (e.g., Beijing South → Shanghai Hongqiao).
  4. Choose a train (G = fastest, D = fast, C = intercity).
  5. Pay with Alipay, WeChat Pay, or an international card.
  6. You'll get a booking confirmation with a QR code.

Option 2: Third-party apps (easier, small fee)

Trip.com and TrainPal sell China train tickets in English with a small service fee. They handle passport registration for you. Good if you want a familiar interface.

Option 3: At the station

You can buy tickets at the station with your passport, but popular routes sell out — book ahead when possible.

Reading your ticket

  • G/D/C = train class (G is fastest)
  • Seat class: 二等座 (second class), 一等座 (first class), 商务座 (business)
  • Car and seat number: e.g., "05车 12A" = car 5, seat 12A
  • Station names: 北京南 = Beijing South, 上海虹桥 = Shanghai Hongqiao

At the station

  1. Arrive 30–45 minutes early for security and boarding.
  2. Security check: bags through X-ray, like an airport but faster.
  3. Find your gate — check the big screens for your train number.
  4. Boarding: scan your passport or QR code at the gate. No paper ticket needed if you booked online.
  5. Find your seat — luggage racks above and at the ends of cars.

Pro tips

  • Book 2–3 days ahead for popular routes (Beijing–Shanghai, holiday periods).
  • Chinese New Year (late Jan/Feb) and Golden Week (Oct 1–7) — book weeks ahead or avoid travel entirely.
  • Food: the dining car serves hot meals; you can also order food to your seat via the app on some trains.
  • Toilets are at the ends of cars — bring your own tissue, it's not always provided.
  • Don't miss your train — tickets are for a specific train and time. Missed tickets can be rebooked with a fee, but it's a hassle.

FAQ

Q: Do I need a Chinese ID to book? A: No — your passport works. Just make sure the name matches exactly.

Q: Can I book same-day? A: Yes, if seats are available. But popular routes sell out, especially on weekends.

Q: Is there WiFi on the train? A: Many G trains have free WiFi, but it can be slow. Download movies before you board.

Q: Can I bring luggage? A: Yes — up to 20 kg for adults, with size limits. Oversized luggage goes in the luggage area at the end of the car.

Q: What if I miss my train? A: Go to the ticket office. You can usually rebook for a later train with a fee (about 20% of the ticket price).

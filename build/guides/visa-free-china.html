China Visa-Free Entry 2026: Complete Guide for Western Travelers

China has dramatically opened up. Since late 2024, citizens of dozens of countries can enter visa-free for up to 30 days for tourism, business, family visits, and transit. This guide explains exactly who qualifies, how long you can stay, and what happens at immigration.

Important: visa policy changes frequently. Always confirm with the Chinese embassy or consulate in your country, or the National Immigration Administration website, before booking flights.

Who can enter China visa-free?

30-day visa-free entry

As of 2026, citizens of 30+ countries can enter China without a visa for up to 30 days. The list includes most of Western Europe, plus Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, and others:

  • Europe: France, Germany, Italy, Netherlands, Spain, Portugal, Switzerland, Ireland, Austria, Belgium, Luxembourg, Denmark, Finland, Sweden, Norway, Iceland, Greece, Poland, Hungary, Czech Republic, Slovakia, Slovenia, Croatia, Bulgaria, Romania, Cyprus, Malta, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Monaco, Andorra, San Marino, Liechtenstein
  • Asia-Pacific: Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Brunei, Australia, New Zealand
  • Others: Russia, and more

The exact list changes — check the latest official list before you travel.

240-hour visa-free transit (10 days)

If your country is not on the 30-day list, you may still qualify for 240-hour (10-day) visa-free transit. Citizens of 54 countries can transit through China for up to 10 days without a visa, provided they:

  • Hold a valid passport with at least 6 months validity
  • Have a confirmed onward ticket to a third country or region (not back to where you came from)
  • Enter and exit through one of the designated ports (most major international airports and some land/sea ports)

This is a great option for a stopover in Beijing, Shanghai, or Guangzhou on the way to a third destination.

How long can I stay?

  • 30-day visa-free: up to 30 days per entry, for tourism, business, family visits, exchanges, and transit.
  • 240-hour transit: up to 10 days, strictly for transit to a third country.

If you need longer, apply for a regular tourist (L) visa at a Chinese embassy or consulate before you go.

What happens at immigration?

  1. Fill in the arrival card (often done digitally at major airports now).
  2. Have your passport ready — make sure it's valid for at least 6 months with blank pages.
  3. Answer a few questions — where you're staying, how long, your return flight. English is usually fine at major airports.
  4. Fingerprints are taken for most foreign visitors.
  5. Get your entry stamp — check the number of days granted on the stamp.

Pro tip: have your hotel address and return flight details written down or on your phone. Immigration officers may ask.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Overstaying — even by a day. Overstays are fined (500 CNY per day, capped) and can get you flagged. Set a reminder for your exit date.
  • Wrong port for transit — 240-hour transit only works at designated ports. Confirm your entry and exit points qualify.
  • Passport validity — many airlines won't let you board if your passport expires within 6 months.
  • Assuming your country is on the list — always verify. The list has changed several times in the last two years.

FAQ

Q: Do I need a visa for a 3-week trip to China? A: If your country is on the 30-day visa-free list, no — 30 days covers most trips. If not, apply for an L (tourist) visa in advance.

Q: Can I extend my visa-free stay? A: Generally no. Visa-free entry is for short stays. For longer stays, apply for a proper visa before arrival.

Q: Does visa-free entry allow me to work? A: No. Visa-free entry is for tourism, business visits, family visits, and transit — not employment. Working without the right visa is illegal.

Q: Can I enter visa-free and leave from a different city? A: Yes, for 30-day visa-free entry you can enter and exit from any port. For 240-hour transit, entry and exit ports must be within the designated region.

Q: What if my country isn't on the list? A: Apply for a tourist visa at your nearest Chinese embassy or consulate. Processing usually takes 4–7 working days.

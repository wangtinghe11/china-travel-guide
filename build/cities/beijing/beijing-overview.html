Beijing Travel Guide 2026: First-Timer's Complete Guide

Beijing is China's imperial heart — the Forbidden City, the Great Wall, and a thousand years of dynastic history, wrapped in a modern megacity of 21 million people. This guide covers everything a first-time Western visitor needs.

Why visit Beijing

  • The Forbidden City — the world's largest palace complex, 980 buildings over 72 hectares.
  • The Great Wall — the most iconic sight in China. Badaling is closest; Mutianyu is less crowded and more scenic.
  • Hutongs — the old alley neighborhoods that survived the demolition waves. Nanluoguxiang is touristy; wander deeper for the real thing.
  • Temple of Heaven — where emperors prayed for good harvests. Go early to see locals doing tai chi and dancing.
  • Food — Peking duck, zhajiangmian (noodle sauce), jianbing (savory crepes), and the night markets.

Getting there

  • Beijing Capital (PEK) — the main international airport, 30–60 min from the city by taxi or Airport Express.
  • Beijing Daxing (PKX) — the newer mega-airport, 45–60 min south. The Daxing Airport Express is fast and cheap.
  • High-speed rail — Beijing South station connects to Shanghai (4.5h), Xi'an (4.5h), and most major cities.

Getting around

  • Metro — 20+ lines, cheap (3–8 CNY), and signs are in English. Use Alipay's transport QR code or buy a transit card.
  • Didi — the Chinese Uber. English version available; pay in-app.
  • Taxis — insist on the meter. Most drivers don't speak English — have your destination written in Chinese.
  • Bikes — Beijing is flat and bike-friendly. HelloBike and Meituan bikes are everywhere.

Where to stay

  • First time: Dongcheng (near Forbidden City) or Wangfujing — walkable to the main sights.
  • Hutong experience: Gulou / Shichahai area — boutique hotels in old alley houses.
  • Business district: CBD / Guomao — modern hotels, great for business travelers.
  • Budget: hostels near the Lama Temple or in the hutongs.

What to see (top 5)

  1. Forbidden City — book tickets online in advance (official site or WeChat). Allow 3–4 hours.
  2. Great Wall — Mutianyu (1.5h from city) is the best balance of scenery and crowds. Go early.
  3. Temple of Heaven — morning tai chi, the Hall of Prayer, and the Echo Wall.
  4. Summer Palace — imperial gardens on Kunming Lake. Half a day.
  5. 798 Art District — contemporary art in a former factory complex.

Food & drink

  • Peking duck — Siji Minfu and Da Dong are famous; a local favorite is 四季民福 near the Forbidden City.
  • Zhajiangmian — Beijing's signature noodle dish, with soybean paste and vegetables.
  • Jianbing — the street breakfast crepe. Get one from a street cart.
  • Night markets — Wangfujing snack street is touristy but fun; try the candied hawthorn sticks.
  • Tea — visit a traditional teahouse in the hutongs.

Sample itinerary (3 days)

  • Day 1: Forbidden City → Jingshan Park (view from the hill) → Wangfujing night market.
  • Day 2: Great Wall (Mutianyu) → back for Peking duck dinner.
  • Day 3: Temple of Heaven → Summer Palace → hutong walk in Gulou.

Practical tips

  • Book the Forbidden City 1–7 days ahead — it sells out, especially in peak season.
  • Carry your passport — needed for some attractions and hotel check-in.
  • Air quality — check the AQI; bring a mask in winter.
  • Language — download a translation app (Alipay has one built in). English is limited outside tourist areas.
  • Money — set up Alipay/WeChat Pay before you arrive (see our payments guide).

FAQ

Q: How many days do I need in Beijing? A: 3–4 days covers the highlights. Add a day for the Great Wall at a relaxed pace.

Q: Is Beijing safe? A: Very safe — one of the safest major cities in the world for tourists. See our safety guide.

Q: Do I need a visa? A: Most Western nationalities get 30 days visa-free. Check our visa guide.

Q: What's the best time to visit? A: April–May and September–October — mild weather, clear skies. Avoid the summer heat and winter smog.

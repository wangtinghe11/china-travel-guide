Shanghai Travel Guide 2026: First-Timer's Complete Guide

Shanghai is China's most international city — art-deco buildings along the Bund, a futuristic skyline across the river in Pudong, and leafy streets in the former French Concession. It's the easiest Chinese city for a first-time Western visitor.

Why visit Shanghai

  • The Bund — the riverside promenade with colonial-era buildings facing the Pudong skyline. Best at night when the lights come on.
  • Yu Garden — a classical Ming-dynasty garden in the old city, with the famous zigzag bridge and teahouse.
  • French Concession — tree-lined streets, art-deco apartments, boutique cafes, and the former residence of Sun Yat-sen.
  • Pudong skyline — the Oriental Pearl Tower, Shanghai Tower (632m), and the financial district. Go up the Shanghai Tower for the view.
  • Food — xiaolongbao (soup dumplings), shengjianbao (pan-fried buns), and the best international dining in China.

Getting there

  • Pudong (PVG) — the main international airport, 45–60 min from the city by Maglev (fastest train in the world, 431 km/h) or taxi.
  • Hongqiao (SHA) — the second airport, closer to the city, mostly domestic and regional flights.
  • High-speed rail — Shanghai Hongqiao station connects to Beijing (4.5h), Hangzhou (45 min), and most major cities.

Getting around

  • Metro — 20+ lines, cheap, and English-friendly. Use Alipay's transport QR code.
  • Didi — works well; English version available.
  • Taxis — meter required. Have addresses in Chinese.
  • Walking — the Bund, French Concession, and old city are very walkable.

Where to stay

  • First time: People's Square / Nanjing Road — central, near the metro.
  • Bund view: the hotels along the Bund (Peninsula, Fairmont Peace) — pricey but iconic.
  • French Concession: boutique hotels and guesthouses on tree-lined streets.
  • Budget: hostels near the old city or Jing'an.

What to see (top 5)

  1. The Bund — walk the promenade at sunset, then watch the skyline light up.
  2. Yu Garden & Old City — the garden, the bazaar, and the City God Temple.
  3. Shanghai Tower — the world's second-tallest building; the observation deck is the best view in the city.
  4. French Concession — Wukang Road, Anfu Road, and the former residences.
  5. Shanghai Museum — world-class Chinese art and bronzes (free, book ahead).

Food & drink

  • Xiaolongbao — soup dumplings. Din Tai Fung is reliable; local favorites are 佳家汤包 and 南翔馒头店.
  • Shengjianbao — pan-fried pork buns, a Shanghai specialty.
  • Benbang cuisine — Shanghai's sweet-and-savory home cooking: 红烧肉 (braised pork), 腌笃鲜 (bamboo shoot soup).
  • Coffee culture — Shanghai has more cafes than any city in China. Try a specialty coffee in the French Concession.
  • Nightlife — the Bund and Xintiandi have the best bars.

Sample itinerary (3 days)

  • Day 1: Yu Garden & Old City → the Bund at sunset → night river cruise.
  • Day 2: Shanghai Tower → Lujiazui → French Concession cafes and boutiques.
  • Day 3: Shanghai Museum → Tianzifang (artsy lanes) → Xintiandi dinner.

Practical tips

  • Book the Shanghai Tower and Museum ahead — queues are long in peak season.
  • Carry your passport — needed for hotel check-in and some attractions.
  • Language — English is more common than elsewhere in China, but a translation app still helps.
  • Money — set up Alipay/WeChat Pay before you arrive (see our payments guide).
  • Day trip — Hangzhou (45 min by train) and Suzhou (30 min) are easy add-ons.

FAQ

Q: How many days do I need in Shanghai? A: 2–3 days covers the highlights. Add a day for a Hangzhou or Suzhou day trip.

Q: Is Shanghai safe? A: Very safe, day and night. See our safety guide.

Q: What's the best time to visit? A: March–May and September–November — mild and pleasant. Summer is hot and humid; winter is cold and damp.

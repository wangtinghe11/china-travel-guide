# How to Pay in China: Alipay, WeChat Pay & Cash for Foreigners

China runs on QR codes. Street vendors, taxis, temples, and even some vending machines expect you to scan a QR code with your phone. The good news: **foreigners can now link international Visa/Mastercard/Amex cards to Alipay and WeChat Pay** — no Chinese bank account needed. Here's exactly how.

## The two apps you need

### Alipay (recommended first)

1. Download **Alipay** from the App Store / Google Play.
2. Register with your foreign phone number and email.
3. Tap **Me → Bank Cards → Add** and link your Visa, Mastercard, or Amex.
4. Verify your identity (passport scan + face check) — required for international cards.
5. Done. You can now scan-to-pay anywhere.

Alipay has an English interface and a built-in translation feature. It also works for the metro in most cities (transport QR code).

### WeChat Pay

1. Download **WeChat** and register.
2. Go to **Me → Services → Wallet → Bank Cards**.
3. Add your international card and complete identity verification.
4. WeChat Pay works in most places Alipay does, and is essential for some small vendors.

**Tip:** set up both. Some merchants only accept one or the other.

## How to pay with your phone

- **Scan to pay (you scan their QR):** open the app, tap "Scan", point at the merchant's QR code, enter the amount, confirm.
- **Show your QR code (they scan yours):** tap "Pay/Collect", show your code, the merchant scans it.
- **In-app payments:** for Didi (ride-hailing), Meituan (food delivery), and train tickets, pay directly in the app.

## When cash still matters

- **Rural areas and small towns** — some vendors are cash-only.
- **Taxis** — most accept QR codes now, but keep small bills as backup.
- **Tipping is not expected** in China, so no need for small change for that.

Carry **200–500 CNY** in small notes as a backup. ATMs at Bank of China, ICBC, and other major banks accept foreign cards (look for the UnionPay/Visa/Mastercard logos). Withdraw in CNY — the ATM rate is usually fair.

## Common problems (and fixes)

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Card rejected when adding | Call your bank — many block China transactions by default. Ask them to enable international use. |
| "Identity verification failed" | Make sure your passport photo is clear and the name matches your card exactly. |
| Small merchants don't accept foreign cards | Use the app's balance — top up via a friend or a Chinese bank card if you have one. |
| App in Chinese | Alipay: switch language in Settings. WeChat: Settings → General → Language. |

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use my foreign credit card directly in China?**
**A:** At hotels and some big stores, yes. But most daily payments (taxis, street food, metro) need Alipay or WeChat Pay.

**Q: Is it safe to link my card to Alipay?**
**A:** Yes — Alipay and WeChat Pay are the two largest payment platforms in the world by transaction volume. Use the official apps and enable fingerprint/Face ID.

**Q: Do I need a Chinese phone number?**
**A:** No. A foreign number works for registration. A Chinese number helps for Didi and some services, but isn't required.

**Q: What about Apple Pay / Google Pay?**
**A:** Apple Pay works with some Chinese bank cards, but not reliably with foreign cards. Don't rely on it.

**Q: Should I exchange cash before arriving?**
**A:** A small amount (200–500 CNY) is useful for backup. Airports and major hotels exchange currency, and ATMs work with foreign cards.

# China's 240-Hour Visa-Free Transit in 2026: Who Qualifies and How to Use It

For millions of Western travelers, China's visa rules used to be the single biggest reason to skip the country. That changed on **December 17, 2024**, when China replaced its 144-hour transit policy with a **240-hour (10-day) visa-free transit** scheme — one of the most generous transit programs in the world. If you're passing through China on the way somewhere else, you may now be able to spend 10 days exploring without ever applying for a visa.

Here's exactly who qualifies, where you can go, and the small print that trips people up.

## What the 240-Hour Transit Policy Actually Is

The policy allows citizens of **57 countries** to enter mainland China **without a visa** for up to 240 hours (10 full days), as long as they are genuinely *transiting* to a third country or region. Entry is permitted through **65 open ports** across **24 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities**, and you're free to cross provincial borders within that permitted area.

The route rule is the heart of it:

**A → China → B**, where A and B are different countries or regions outside mainland China.

- ✅ **UK → Beijing (5 days) → Tokyo** — qualifies
- ✅ **US → Shanghai (3 days) → Hong Kong** — qualifies (Hong Kong counts as a third region)
- ❌ **Germany → Guangzhou → Germany** — a round trip, does not qualify
- ❌ **Mongolia → Hohhot → Japan** — Inner Mongolia's specific port isn't on the eligible list, so this route fails

## The 57 Eligible Countries

The list covers most of the Western world:

- **25 Schengen countries**: Austria, Belgium, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland
- **15 other European countries**: Russia, United Kingdom, Ireland, Cyprus, Bulgaria, Romania, Ukraine, Serbia, Croatia, Bosnia-Herzegovina, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Albania, Belarus, Monaco
- **6 American countries**: United States, Canada, Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile
- **2 Oceania countries**: Australia, New Zealand
- **9 Asian countries**: South Korea, Japan, Singapore, Brunei, UAE, Qatar, Indonesia, Kyrgyzstan, Vietnam

If your passport isn't on this list, the standard tourist (L) visa is still your route — see our [China visa guide](/guides/china-visa-guide/) for the full application process.

## Where You Can Go: 24 Provinces, 65 Ports

The policy is not limited to a single city. Once admitted, you can travel across the entire approved area, which covers 24 provinces and regions. That includes the big-ticket destinations and then some:

- **Beijing, Shanghai, Tianjin, Chongqing** — the four municipalities
- **Guangdong** — Guangzhou, Shenzhen, and the Greater Bay Area, with ferries and trains to Hong Kong and Macau
- **Sichuan** — Chengdu, Leshan, Yibin, and more (home of the pandas and Sichuan hotpot)
- **Yunnan** — Kunming, Dali, Lijiang, Xishuangbanna
- **Guangxi** — Nanning, Guilin, Yangshuo (the karst landscapes of the Li River)
- **Hunan** — Changsha and Zhangjiajie (the floating mountains of *Avatar* fame)
- **Shaanxi** — Xi'an and the Terracotta Army
- **Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Fujian, Shandong, Hubei, Henan, Anhui, Hainan, Liaoning, Heilongjiang, Shanxi, Guizhou, Jiangxi, Hebei**

Entry ports include Beijing Capital and Daxing airports, Shanghai Pudong and Hongqiao, Guangzhou Baiyun, Shenzhen Bao'an, Chengdu Tianfu and Shuangliu, Xi'an Xianyang, Kunming Changshui, Hangzhou Xiaoshan, Qingdao Jiaodong, and the Hong Kong West Kowloon high-speed rail station among many others.

**One critical rule**: the international flights carrying you *into* and *out of* China must not make a stopover in a city outside the approved area. A direct flight is always the safest choice.

## How the 240 Hours Are Counted (The Best-Kept Secret)

This is the detail most travelers discover too late — in a good way. **The 240-hour clock does not start when you land. It starts at 00:00 on the day *after* your arrival.**

So if you land at 6:00 am on July 1, your window begins at midnight on July 2 and runs to 23:59 on July 11. In practice that gives you up to **about 258 hours** — nearly 11 days. Arriving late in the evening is even better, since you lose less of the first day.

There is **no limit** on how many times you can use the policy, and because immigration counts your *scheduled* arrival and departure times, a flight delay won't void your eligibility.

## Documents You Need at the Border

Getting admitted is a paperwork exercise, and it's straightforward if you prepare:

1. **Passport** valid for at least **3 months** from your date of entry
2. **Confirmed onward ticket** to a third country or region, with a fixed date
3. **Arrival Card** — since November 20, 2025 you can fill this in **online before boarding** via the National Immigration Administration's "NIA 12367" app or its WeChat / Alipay mini program. Paper cards are still available at the port if you'd rather do it on arrival
4. **Hotel booking or address** — you'll be asked where you're staying

At immigration, look for the dedicated **240-hour visa-free transit counter**. Tell the officer clearly that you want to use the transit policy (especially if you also hold a Chinese visa but don't want to use it this time). They'll stamp a stay permit into your passport showing your allowed period.

> ⚠️ You can be refused if you can't show the required documents, if your route doesn't qualify, or if you've previously overstayed in another country. Book refundable flights until you're confident your itinerary is eligible.

## Registration and Practical Tips

- **Hotel stays**: your hotel handles accommodation registration for you automatically. If you stay in a private apartment or with friends, you must register at the local police station **within 24 hours** of entry.
- **Stay inside the approved area**: crossing into a non-eligible province is treated as overstaying, even by a day.
- **Carry your onward ticket**: random checks happen at hotels and when booking domestic transport.
- **Use the time wisely**: the high-speed rail network makes 3–5 city itineraries realistic in 10 days. A classic route is Beijing → Xi'an → Shanghai, or Shanghai → Guilin → Zhangjiajie for scenery.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- ❌ Booking a round trip *to and from* the same country and assuming it counts as transit
- ❌ Choosing an arrival port that isn't on the eligible list
- ❌ Flying out via a stopover that returns you to your origin country (this turns the trip into a round trip)
- ❌ Forgetting the 24-hour police registration if you're not in a hotel
- ❌ Assuming visa-free entry applies to everyone — the 30-day unilateral visa-free scheme and the 240-hour transit have *different* country lists

## 240-Hour Transit vs. the 30-Day Visa-Free Scheme

Don't confuse the transit policy with China's **unilateral visa-free entry**, which is a different program entirely. Under the unilateral scheme, citizens of a smaller set of countries (largely European nations plus Australia, New Zealand, Japan, and South Korea) can enter China for up to **30 days** for tourism with no onward ticket and no "third country" requirement at all.

The two lists overlap but are **not identical**, and the rules are different:

- **240-hour transit**: 57 countries, requires A → China → B, no entry fee, up to 10 days, must exit to a third country
- **30-day unilateral visa-free**: shorter country list, no transit requirement, up to 30 days, can be a round trip

If you qualify for the 30-day scheme, that's usually the simpler option. If you don't — as is the case for US, Canadian, and most Latin American passport holders — the 240-hour transit is often the best workaround. Check both lists against your nationality before you book.

## Sample 10-Day Transit Itineraries

Because the approved area spans 24 provinces and the high-speed rail network is fast and cheap, a 240-hour window supports genuinely ambitious trips:

- **Classic first-timers**: Beijing (Great Wall, Forbidden City, 4 days) → high-speed rail to Xi'an (Terracotta Army, 2 days) → fly to Shanghai (The Bund, 3 days)
- **Nature-focused**: Shanghai → Guilin and Yangshuo (Li River karst) → Zhangjiajie (Avatar mountains)
- **Food-focused**: Chengdu (pandas, Sichuan hotpot, teahouses) → Chongqing (hotpot, the 8D skyline) → Guangzhou (dim sum and Cantonese cuisine)

Just remember your onward flight must depart from a port inside the approved area, and you cannot stray into a province that isn't on the list.

## The Bottom Line

If you hold a passport from one of the 57 eligible countries and can route your trip as **A → China → B**, the 240-hour visa-free transit is the cheapest and easiest way to see China in 2026. Ten days — effectively closer to eleven — is enough for Beijing, Xi'an, and Shanghai, or a deep dive into Sichuan or Yunnan. Just nail down the documents, confirm your ports, and watch the approved-area boundary.

# What to Eat in Hangzhou: West Lake Dishes, Noodles & Longjing Tea

**The short answer**: try West Lake vinegar fish (西湖醋鱼) and Dongpo pork (东坡肉) at Waipo Jia (外婆家) or Lücha (绿茶), eat pian'erchuan (片儿川) noodles for breakfast, and drink Longjing tea in the village where it grows. Hangzhou food is light, fresh, and slightly sweet. Budget ¥60–100 per person for a sit-down meal.

## The Must-Try List

### 1. West Lake Vinegar Fish (西湖醋鱼) — the famous dish
A whole fish steamed and dressed with a sweet-and-sour vinegar sauce. The name comes from West Lake, and the dish is a Hangzhou institution. It's an acquired taste — the vinegar is assertive — but it's the dish to try.

- **Where**: Waipo Jia (外婆家) and Lücha (绿茶) — where locals actually eat.
- **Price**: ¥60–100 per person.

### 2. Dongpo Pork (东坡肉) — melt-in-your-mouth braised pork
Named after the poet Su Dongpo, who served in Hangzhou: a square of pork belly braised for hours in soy, sugar, and wine until it's glossy and tender. Rich, sweet, and unforgettable.

- **Where**: Same as above — Waipo Jia and Lücha.
- **Price**: ¥40–60 per dish.

### 3. Pian'erchuan (片儿川) — the local breakfast
A bowl of bamboo-shoot noodles in a light pork broth — the Hangzhou breakfast staple. ¥15–20 a bowl, and the perfect start to a West Lake day.

- **Where**: Any local noodle shop.
- **Price**: ¥15–20.

### 4. Xiaolongbao (小笼包) — the Hangzhou version
Hangzhou's xiaolongbao has a **leavened (发面) wrapper** — thicker and breadier than Shanghai's thin-skinned version. The old shops **Zhiweiguan (知味观)** and **Xinfeng (新丰)** are the classics.

- **Where**: Zhiweiguan (知味观) and Xinfeng (新丰).
- **Price**: ¥10–20 for a basket.

### 5. Longjing Tea (龙井茶) — the city's liquid soul
Hangzhou is the home of Longjing, China's most famous green tea. The real experience: sit in a teahouse in **Longjing Village (龙井村)** and pay **¥50–100 for a cup of fresh Mingqian (pre-Qingming) tea**. Don't buy loose tea at the scenic spots — it's overpriced and often not real Longjing.

- **Where**: Longjing Village (龙井村).
- **Price**: ¥50–100 per cup.

### 6. Silk Museum & Sweet Treats
The **China Silk Museum (中国丝绸博物馆)** is free — and if you want to buy silk scarves, head to the **Silk City market (丝绸城)** and bargain. For dessert, try **osmanthus cake (桂花糕)** — especially lovely in September–October when the whole city smells of osmanthus.

## Local vs Tourist: Where to Eat

- **Skip**: Restaurants right on West Lake's main tourist paths — overpriced and average.
- **Go**: Waipo Jia (外婆家) and Lücha (绿茶) — the local favorites. He Fang Street (河坊街) for snacks and souvenirs.
- **Tea**: Longjing Village, not the scenic spots.

## Street Food vs Sit-Down

- **Street food (¥10–30)**: Pian'erchuan, xiaolongbao, and osmanthus cakes.
- **Sit-down (¥60–100)**: West Lake vinegar fish, Dongpo pork, and a proper Hangzhou meal.

## Practical Tips

- **Cashless city**: Hangzhou is Alipay's home city — the whole city is cashless. Make sure your Alipay is set up.
- **No tipping**: Tipping is not expected.
- **Tea etiquette**: When someone pours you tea, tap two fingers on the table to say thanks.

## FAQ

**What food is Hangzhou famous for?** West Lake vinegar fish, Dongpo pork, pian'erchuan noodles, and Longjing tea.

**Where do locals eat?** Waipo Jia and Lücha — good value and consistently good.

**What's the difference between Hangzhou and Shanghai xiaolongbao?** Hangzhou's has a leavened wrapper — thicker and breadier. Try Zhiweiguan or Xinfeng.

**Is Hangzhou food spicy?** No — light, fresh, and slightly sweet.

**Where can I try real Longjing tea?** Longjing Village, ¥50–100 for a cup of fresh Mingqian tea.

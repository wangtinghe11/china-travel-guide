# Chengdu Travel Guide 2026: Itineraries, Transport & Insider Tips

Chengdu is the laid-back capital of Sichuan — home to giant pandas, world-famous hotpot, and a teahouse culture that slows time down. Here's the complete guide.

## Day 1: Pandas & Teahouses

**Morning**: **Giant Panda Base** — arrive at the **7:30 AM opening** and head straight to the nursery. By 9 AM the pandas are all asleep, so the first hour is everything. Allow 3 hours.

**Afternoon**: **People's Park (人民公园)** — the **Heming Teahouse (鹤鸣茶社)** is old-Chengdu daily life: a bowl of gaiwan tea, a snack, and a **ear-cleaning (掏耳朵)** session. It's a surreal, wonderful ritual.

**Evening**: **Kuanzhai Alley (宽窄巷子)** — worth a stroll for the atmosphere, but eat elsewhere. Head to **Kuixinglou Street (奎星楼街)** for dinner — that's where locals actually go.

## Day 2: Hotpot & City Life

**Morning**: **Wenshu Monastery** (peaceful, great vegetarian food) or **Sichuan Museum** (free, Shu culture).

**Afternoon**: Explore the city's parks and street life. Chengdu is cloudy and rains often — carry an umbrella.

**Evening**: **Hotpot night** — the city's religion. Order a **yuanyang (half-and-half) pot** if you're not a spice veteran: even "mild" Sichuan hotpot can be brutal for Western palates. Try **rabbit head (兔头)** and **chuanchuan (串串)** — skewers charged by the stick, usually ¥50–80 per person.

## Day 3 (Optional): Dujiangyan

**Dujiangyan** is 30 minutes by high-speed rail — a 2,000-year-old irrigation system still in use today, and genuinely awe-inspiring. Combine with a visit to **Mount Qingcheng** (Taoist mountain) or push on to **Leshan** for the giant Buddha.

**Skip the far-flung parks**: Jiuzhaigou and Daocheng Yading are stunning but a multi-day trip from Chengdu. With only 3–4 days, spend them on Dujiangyan and Leshan instead.

## Where to Eat Like a Local

- **Hotpot**: The spicier the better for locals — but you don't have to prove anything. Yuanyang pot, always.
- **Chuanchuan (串串)**: Skewers cooked in a bubbling pot, charged by the stick (¥50–80 per person). Fun, social, cheap.
- **Rabbit head (兔头)**: A Chengdu specialty — spicy, savory, and surprisingly meaty. Try one.
- **The snack trio**: Dan dan noodles (担担面), Zhong dumplings (钟水饺), and Long Chao Shou (龙抄手) — the three classic Chengdu snacks, all worth a try.
- **Teahouse snacks**: Peanuts, sunflower seeds, and a gaiwan of jasmine tea at Heming Teahouse.

## Practical Tips

- **Panda Base**: 7:30 AM opening, no exceptions. The pandas are active for about 90 minutes, then it's nap time.
- **Airports**: **Tianfu (TFU)** is far — 1 hour by metro. **Shuangliu (CTU)** is close. Don't book a hotel near the wrong one!
- **Metro**: Scan the QR code at the gates to enter. For the Panda Base, take the metro then the shuttle bus.
- **Teahouses**: Teahouse culture is everywhere — find any 坝坝茶 (open-air tea stall) and sit for an afternoon. It's the Chengdu way.
- **Nightlife**: Jiuyanqiao (九眼桥) bar street gets lively at night — a fun spot for a drink after dinner.
- **Weather**: Cloudy and rainy year-round — always carry an umbrella. Summer is hot and humid.
- **Language**: Learn "bashi" (巴适, comfortable/great) and "yaode" (要得, OK/sure) — locals light up when foreigners use them.
- **Metro**: Excellent, cheap, and English-signed. Covers all major sights.

## Where to Stay

- **Chunxi Road / Taikoo Li** — central shopping district
- **Kuanzhai Alley area** — atmospheric, near the old lanes
- **People's Park area** — local life, teahouses nearby

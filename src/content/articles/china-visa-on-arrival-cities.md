# China Visa on Arrival 2026: Cities, Ports, Eligibility and Costs

Most travelers assume that getting into China means either qualifying for visa-free entry or spending weeks at a consulate. But there is a third, oddly under-used path: **the China visa on arrival**, known locally as the *port visa*. It exists, it is cheap, and for the right itinerary it is far faster than a regular application.

The catch is that it is available in only a handful of places. This guide covers exactly where, who qualifies in 2026, what it costs, and how it compares to the better-known 240-hour visa-free transit.

## What "China Visa on Arrival" Actually Means

Two different things get called "visa on arrival" in China, and confusing them is the most common mistake:

1. **The Special Economic Zone Tourist Visa (port visa)** — a genuine visa issued at the border in **Shenzhen, Zhuhai, and Xiamen**, costing CNY 130.
2. **Hainan's 30-day visa-free access** — not a visa at all, but a visa-free entry scheme open to citizens of 61 countries.

There is **no nationwide visa-on-arrival program**. You cannot simply fly into Beijing or Shanghai and expect a stamp at immigration. The regular tourist (L) visa, the 240-hour transit policy, or one of the unilateral visa-free schemes are the routes for everywhere else.

There is also a separate **emergency port visa** system, now operating at 99 ports across 72 cities, for travelers who need to enter urgently for business, family, or medical reasons and hold an invitation letter from an accredited Chinese organization. That is a business tool, not a tourist one — do not count on it for a holiday.

## Where You Can Get a Port Visa in 2026

### Shenzhen — 5 Days

Shenzhen is the most commonly used port-visa entry point, because millions of travelers cross from **Hong Kong** every year.

- **Eligible ports**: Luohu (Lo Wu), Huanggang, and Shekou
- **Stay**: 5 days
- **Cost**: CNY 130 (about $18)
- **Restriction**: you may travel **within Shenzhen only**. Crossing to Guangzhou, Dongguan, or anywhere else in Guangdong is not permitted

This last point catches people out constantly. There are roughly ten crossings between Shenzhen and Hong Kong, but only **three** issue port visas. If you walk across at Futian or Shenzhen Bay expecting a visa, you will be turned back — and you will have already left Hong Kong, which means a trip back.

### Zhuhai — 3 Days

- **Eligible ports**: Gongbei, Hengqin, Jiuzhou Port, and the **Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge Zhuhai Port**
- **Stay**: 3 days
- **Cost**: CNY 130

Zhuhai is the quiet option. It borders Macau rather than Hong Kong, so the crowds are lighter. The HKZMB crossing is particularly convenient if you are arriving by the bridge shuttle bus from Hong Kong.

### Xiamen — 3 or 5 Days

- **Eligible ports**: Xiamen Gaoqi International Airport, Xiamen International Cruise Center, and Wutong Passenger Terminal
- **Stay**: 3 or 5 days, depending on your nationality and itinerary
- **Cost**: CNY 130

Xiamen is the only one of the three reachable by **air** as well as by land and sea, which makes it genuinely useful for travelers flying into Fujian from Southeast Asia.

### Hainan Province — 30 Days, Free

Hainan is the outlier and, for many travelers, the best deal of all. Citizens of **61 countries** can enter for up to **30 days** visa-free through any open port in the province, including Haikou Meilan and Sanya Phoenix airports.

The 61 countries include the United States, Canada, Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Australia, New Zealand, the 25 Schengen nations, the UK, Russia, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, the UAE, Qatar, and more.

**The rules to know:**
- The 30 days are counted from 00:00 on the day *after* your arrival
- Permitted purposes are tourism, business, exchange visits, family visits, exhibitions, and sports competitions — **not** study or work
- You must arrive and depart **within Hainan**. Continuing to mainland China requires a separate visa
- You may be asked for a round-trip ticket and hotel bookings

Hainan is a full province, not a city — Sanya's beaches, Haikou's old town, and the rainforest around Wuzhishan are all fair game for a month.

## Who Is Eligible — and Who Isn't

For the Shenzhen, Zhuhai, and Xiamen port visas, passports from most countries are accepted, including the **United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, and most of Europe**. You just fill in a form at the visa office at the border, take a photo, and pay the fee. The visa is typically issued within **5 to 10 minutes**, longer if the office is busy.

**Not eligible** for the port visa are passport holders from: Afghanistan, Algeria, Bahrain, India, Egypt, Iran, Iraq, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Libya, Nigeria, Oman, Pakistan, Palestine, Qatar, Somalia, Sri Lanka, Syria, Sudan, Turkey, and Yemen. Citizens of some of these countries may qualify only if they can show a previously issued Chinese visa.

Some nationalities — the Philippines and Indonesia are cited examples — may be asked to show proof of a prior Chinese visa. It helps, but does not guarantee approval.

Note the important overlap: citizens of the **75-plus nationalities covered by China's unilateral visa-free scheme** (much of Europe, plus Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, and others) can simply enter China without any visa at all. If you hold one of those passports, a port visa is unnecessary — you are already visa-free for up to 30 days.

## Port Visa vs. 240-Hour Transit vs. 30-Day Visa-Free

The three entry routes serve different itineraries:

| Route | Who | Duration | Onward ticket to third country? | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Port visa** (Shenzhen/Zhuhai/Xiamen) | Most nationalities | 3–5 days | No | One city only |
| **240-hour transit** | 57 countries | Up to 10 days | Yes, required | 24 provinces |
| **30-day unilateral visa-free** | 75+ nationalities | Up to 30 days | No | All of mainland China |
| **Hainan visa-free access** | 61 countries | 30 days | No | Hainan only |

**How to choose:**

- Flying **US → Shanghai → Tokyo**? Use the 240-hour transit — ten days, no visa, no fee.
- Holding an **Australian, British, or EU passport** and coming round-trip? The 30-day unilateral scheme is simplest.
- Crossing by land from **Hong Kong into Shenzhen** without an onward flight to a third country? The **port visa** is your answer.
- Want a **month of beach and rainforest**? Hainan's visa-free access beats everything else.

US, Canadian, and most Latin American passport holders get no unilateral visa-free entry, so for them the realistic choices are the port visa, the 240-hour transit, or a full L visa.

## Practical Steps at the Border

1. **Look for the visa office** — signage at eligible ports reads "Visa on Arrival" or "Port Visa." At Luohu and Huanggang it is a dedicated room off the main arrivals hall.
2. **Complete the application form** there. Bring a pen; they are not always provided.
3. **Have a photo taken** on site — no need to bring one.
4. **Pay CNY 130**. Cash in renminbi is safest; not every office takes foreign cards.
5. **Wait 5–10 minutes** for the visa to be printed and stamped.
6. **Register your accommodation** — hotels handle this for you; private stays require a police station visit within 24 hours.

Expect the whole process, including queuing, to take **30 to 60 minutes**. Arrive outside peak hours if you can. Late Friday afternoons and Sunday evenings at Luohu are the worst.

## Mistakes That Cost Travelers Money

- ❌ **Crossing at the wrong Shenzhen port.** Only Luohu, Huanggang, and Shekou issue visas.
- ❌ **Assuming you can travel onward.** A Shenzhen port visa is worthless in Guangzhou.
- ❌ **Planning a multi-city trip on a port visa.** If you want Beijing plus Shanghai, get a proper visa or use the 240-hour transit.
- ❌ **Showing up without a valid passport.** It must be valid well beyond your intended stay — three months minimum is the safe rule.
- ❌ **Arriving at Hainan expecting to reach the mainland.** You cannot. The visa-free access is Hainan-only.
- ❌ **Forgetting that port visas are single-entry.** Step out of Shenzhen to Hong Kong and your visa is finished; you would have to apply again.

## When You Should Just Get a Regular Visa

If your plans involve **more than one city outside the three port-visa zones**, a stay **longer than five days**, or **multiple entries**, the port visa will not serve you. Apply for the standard L tourist visa instead — roughly **$140 for US citizens**, with 4–5 business days standard processing and 10-year multiple-entry validity for US passport holders. Our [China visa guide](/guides/china-visa-guide/) walks through the full application.

If you are transiting to a third country, the [240-hour visa-free transit](/guides/china-visa-free-transit-240-hours/) is almost always the better option — it gives you ten days and 24 provinces instead of five days in one city.

## Concluding Thoughts

The China visa on arrival is a real, working option in 2026 — just a narrow one. It is a **city-level entry permit**, not a national one: shop in Shenzhen for five days, wander Zhuhai for three, or explore Xiamen for up to five, all for CNY 130 and ten minutes at a counter. Hainan stands apart with 30 visa-free days for citizens of 61 countries, which is the single best-value entry route in the country for anyone heading to the tropics.

Choose the route that matches your passport and your itinerary — port visa, 240-hour transit, unilateral visa-free, or a full L visa — and confirm the specifics with the National Immigration Administration or your nearest Chinese embassy before you book. Policies in this area have moved quickly for several years, and the details that matter most are the ones printed on the day you travel.

# What to Eat in Guilin & Yangshuo: Rice Noodles, Beer Fish & Local Specialties

**The short answer**: eat Guilin rice noodles (桂林米粉) for breakfast — dry-mixed first, then add broth — and Yangshuo beer fish (啤酒鱼) for dinner. Try Longji bamboo rice at the terraces. Budget ¥5–8 for noodles, ¥60–100 for beer fish.

## The Must-Try List

### 1. Guilin Rice Noodles (桂林米粉) — the breakfast of Guilin
The local breakfast: rice noodles topped with braised pork, peanuts, and pickled vegetables, served **dry-mixed first (干拌), then you add broth (加汤)**. The broth comes on the side — add it after you've eaten most of the noodles. **Chongshan (崇善)** and **Shiji (石记)** are the local institutions.

- **Where**: Chongshan (崇善) and Shiji (石记) — look for the queues.
- **Price**: ¥5–8 a bowl. Yes, that cheap.

### 2. Beer Fish (啤酒鱼) — Yangshuo's signature
Fish braised in beer with tomatoes, peppers, and ginger — a Yangshuo specialty. The fish is fresh from the Li River, and the sauce is rich and slightly sweet. **Choose a restaurant with clear prices on the menu** — some places charge by the fish's weight and the bill can surprise you.

- **Where**: Yangshuo restaurants along West Street and the Yulong River.
- **Price**: ¥60–100 per person.

### 3. Longji Bamboo Rice (竹筒饭) — the terraces' gift
Sticky rice cooked inside bamboo tubes with pork and mushrooms — a specialty of the Longji Rice Terraces. Eat it at a village guesthouse while watching the sunset over the terraces.

- **Where**: Longji Rice Terraces villages.
- **Price**: ¥20–30.

### 4. Guilin Three Treasures (桂林三宝) — the souvenirs
**Sanhua wine (三花酒)** — a rice liquor; **chili sauce (辣椒酱)** — Guilin's famous fermented chili; and **fermented bean curd (豆腐乳)**. Plus **osmanthus cakes (桂花糕)** — Guilin is the osmanthus capital. All make great gifts.

- **Where**: Any supermarket or souvenir shop.
- **Price**: ¥10–50.

### 5. Yangshuo Countryside Food
The countryside around Yangshuo has excellent farm-to-table food: stir-fried river snails, bamboo shoots, and free-range chicken. Ask your guesthouse to recommend a local farm restaurant.

## Local vs Tourist: Where to Eat

- **Skip**: Restaurants on West Street's main strip — touristy and overpriced.
- **Go**: Side streets, farm restaurants in the countryside, and any shop with a queue of locals.
- **Beer fish warning**: Always check the menu prices before ordering — some places charge by weight.

## Street Food vs Sit-Down

- **Street food (¥5–20)**: Guilin rice noodles, grilled river fish, and osmanthus cakes.
- **Sit-down (¥60–100)**: Beer fish and farm-to-table countryside meals.

## Practical Tips

- **Cash**: Small villages and farm restaurants may not take cards — carry some cash.
- **Spice**: Guilin food is mildly spicy — the rice noodles come with pickled chilies on the side. Add to taste.
- **No tipping**: Tipping is not expected.

## FAQ

**What food is Guilin famous for?** Guilin rice noodles, Yangshuo beer fish, and Longji bamboo rice.

**How do you eat Guilin rice noodles properly?** Dry-mixed first, then add broth. Chongshan and Shiji are the classics.

**What is beer fish?** Fish braised in beer with tomatoes and peppers — Yangshuo's signature. Choose a restaurant with clear prices.

**Is Guilin food spicy?** Mildly — not as spicy as Sichuan, but chili is common.

**What should I buy as souvenirs?** The Guilin three treasures: Sanhua wine, chili sauce, and fermented bean curd — plus osmanthus cakes.

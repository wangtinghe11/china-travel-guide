# What to Eat in Guangzhou: Dim Sum, Sweet Soups & Cantonese Classics

**The short answer**: start the day with dim sum (yum cha), eat rice rolls (肠粉) for a quick breakfast, and finish with double-skin milk (双皮奶) at Nanxin. For dinner, get char siu (烧腊) from a roadside shop. Cantonese food is fresh, light, and sweet — the opposite of Sichuan. Budget ¥60–100 for dim sum, ¥20–40 for street food.

## The Must-Try List

### 1. Dim Sum (点心) — the Cantonese breakfast ritual
The heart of Cantonese cuisine: small plates of steamed and fried dishes served with tea. The classics: **har gow (虾饺, shrimp dumplings)**, **siu mai (烧卖)**, **char siu bao (叉烧包, BBQ pork buns)**, and **cheung fun (肠粉, rice rolls)**. The ritual is called **yum cha (饮茶)** — "drinking tea" — and it's a social event that can last all morning.

- **Where**: **Dian Dou De (点都德)** and **Tao Tao Ju (陶陶居)** are the famous names. Go before 10 AM to beat the queue.
- **Price**: ¥60–100 per person.

### 2. Rice Rolls (肠粉) — silky, savory, perfect
Steamed rice noodle sheets folded around shrimp, beef, or egg, drizzled with sweet soy sauce. **Yinji (银记)** is the classic chain — silky, savory, and perfect for breakfast.

- **Where**: Yinji (银记) — multiple branches across the city.
- **Price**: ¥10–20.

### 3. Double-Skin Milk (双皮奶) & Sweet Soups (糖水)
The legendary dessert: a custard-like milk pudding with two layers of skin. **Nanxin (南信)** is the most famous — still queuing at 10 PM. Also try **mango pomelo sago (杨枝甘露)** — a refreshing mango, pomelo, and sago dessert. **Baihua (百花)** is another old favorite.

- **Where**: Nanxin (南信) on Shangxiajiu Pedestrian Street; Baihua (百花) nearby.
- **Price**: ¥15–30.

### 4. Char Siu (烧腊) — the perfect Cantonese meal
Roast meats — char siu (BBQ pork), siu yuk (crispy pork belly), and roast duck — hanging in shop windows. The local way: **"cut meat" (斩料)** from a roadside shop and eat it with rice. Cheap, fast, and delicious.

- **Where**: Any busy roadside shop. Point at what looks good.
- **Price**: ¥20–40 with rice.

### 5. Herbal Tea (凉茶) — bitter but effective
The Cantonese answer to the heat: bitter herbal brews that "cool" the body. **Huang Zhenlong (黄振龙)** and **Wang Laoji (王老吉)** are the famous brands. It's an acquired taste — but a true Guangzhou experience.

- **Where**: Herbal tea shops on every corner.
- **Price**: ¥5–15.

### 6. Noodles & Old Favorites
**Baohua noodles (宝华面店)** — wonton noodles and beef brisket noodles, a local institution. And for a full Cantonese breakfast, add **congee (粥)** with youtiao (fried dough sticks).

## Local vs Tourist: Where to Eat

- **Skip**: Restaurants right at the Canton Tower and tourist malls.
- **Go**: Shangxiajiu (上下九) and Beijing Road (北京路) side streets, Xiguan (西关) old neighborhood, and any shop with a queue of locals.
- **Old favorites**: Nanxin (南信), Baihua (百花), Yinji (银记), Baohua (宝华面店) — all local institutions that have been serving for decades.

## Street Food vs Sit-Down

- **Street food (¥10–30)**: Rice rolls, char siu with rice, sweet soups, and egg waffles (鸡蛋仔).
- **Sit-down (¥60–100)**: Dim sum and full Cantonese banquets. Dim sum is a morning-to-noon ritual — go with friends and order a spread.

## Local Etiquette

- **Tea first**: In Cantonese restaurants, the first thing you do is rinse your chopsticks with tea and pour tea for your companions. Tap two fingers on the table to say thanks when someone pours for you.
- **"M goi" (唔该)**: Say it for "thank you" — locals really appreciate it when visitors try Cantonese.
- **No tipping**: Tipping is not expected in Guangzhou.

## FAQ

**What food is Guangzhou famous for?** Dim sum, rice rolls, double-skin milk, char siu, and herbal tea — the heart of Cantonese cuisine.

**What is dim sum and how does it work?** Small plates of steamed and fried dishes with tea. Order har gow, siu mai, and char siu bao. Go before 10 AM.

**Where should I try dim sum?** Dian Dou De and Tao Tao Ju — about ¥60–100 per person.

**Is Guangzhou food spicy?** No — Cantonese food is fresh, light, and sweet. The heat is the weather, not the food.

**What's the best dessert?** Double-skin milk at Nanxin and mango pomelo sago — the sweet soup shops are still queuing at 10 PM.

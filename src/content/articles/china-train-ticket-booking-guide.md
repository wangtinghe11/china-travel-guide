# How to Book China Train Tickets as a Foreigner (12306 Guide)

China's high-speed rail is the easiest long-distance transport in the country, and the booking system is genuinely open to foreign passports. The catch is that 12306 was designed for 1.4 billion domestic users first: it assumes a Chinese ID number, phone number and payment app. None of that is fatal, but each assumption is a step where first-time visitors get stuck — usually at passport verification, or three weeks out when they discover the ticket they want does not exist yet. This guide walks the official route end to end, with the real fee tables and the station-level details that decide whether you make the train or watch it leave.

## The 15-Day Window Changes Everything

The single most common planning error is assuming you can buy a China train ticket months ahead, the way you would a European or Japanese rail ticket. You cannot. China Railway's official ticketing help page states the online presale period is **[15 days including the day of travel](https://help.12306.cn/cms/answer/h5/001531826595438842083440b580b2e0)**, with station and agency counters on the same window. Regional railway companies can adjust it temporarily, which is why you occasionally see 14 or 16 days quoted.

Two consequences follow, and they matter more than any app recommendation:

- **Set a reminder for day 15, not day 30.** If you want four seats on Beijing South–Shanghai Hongqiao on a Friday in October, the booking page will simply be empty until the window opens.
- **Release is staggered by station, not by route.** China Railway publishes 41 separate release times running from 08:00 to 18:00 in 15-minute steps, and the departure station decides which one applies to your ticket.

That second point is the detail almost no English guide carries. From China Railway's published **[station release schedule](https://www.12306.cn/mormhweb/zxdt/201411/t20141126_2316.html)**: Beijing West and Beijing Fengtai release at **08:00**, Chengdu East at 08:45, Shenzhen North at 09:15, Guangzhou South at 10:15, Xi'an North at 10:30, Hangzhou East at 10:45, Chongqing North at 11:00, **Beijing South at 12:45**, Shanghai Hongqiao at 13:45, Changsha South at 15:45, Zhangjiajie West at 16:30, Guilin North at 17:15 and Fenghuang Ancient Town at 17:30.

Why does this matter? Because the departure station's clock governs the whole ticket. A Beijing South → Shanghai ticket opens at 12:45 while you are still asleep in a New York hotel; Shanghai Hongqiao → Beijing South goes on sale at 13:45 even though Beijing South is on the other end. On the flagship corridor in peak season, being 90 minutes late can mean second class is gone and only first class remains.

## Step 1: Register With Your Passport, Not Your Nickname

Use the official English site, [12306.cn/en](https://www.12306.cn/en/index.html), or the 12306 app with the language switched to English. Both are run by China Railway, which states plainly on its [official FAQ](https://www.12306.cn/en/faq.html) that it will not resolve ticketing problems created on other websites — anything you book elsewhere is your own risk.

Register with the passport you will actually carry. Enter your name exactly as it appears in the machine-readable zone — surname and given names in the same order and spelling as the passport, no middle-name improvisation, no accents. A mismatch between the booking record and the document is what triggers a red gate at the station, and gate staff cannot override it on the spot.

One structural limit worth knowing before you start: the English site accepts **valid foreign passports only**. If you hold a Chinese resident ID, a Home Return Permit for Hong Kong/Macau or a Taiwan travel pass, the English interface will reject you and you need the Chinese-language site instead. This is the reverse of the usual assumption that the English site is more forgiving.

## Step 2: Clear Passport Verification (the Step People Skip)

Real-name ticketing is mandatory on China's rail network under the Ministry of Transport's [ticket real-name rules](https://www.nra.gov.cn/xxgk/gkml/ztjg/gfzd/bmgz/202211/t20221125_339122.shtml): the ticket is tied to the document, and the document is checked at the gate. Your passport is not just ID here, it is the ticket itself.

After you add yourself — and any travel companions — to the "My Passengers" list, your status may show as **"to be verified"** (12306 explains this state in its [English FAQ](https://www.12306.cn/en/faq.html)). This is not an error: the system holds your passport details but has not yet matched them to a physical document. You can still complete the purchase. What you cannot do is collect a paper ticket until you take the **original passport to a station ticket counter** and have staff verify it; the status then changes to "ready to be verified."

Practical read: if you are boarding with your passport, "to be verified" is fine. If you need a paper ticket for a reimbursement receipt, budget 20 minutes at a station counter before departure day. One passport can also hold only one ticket for the same train on the same date, so you cannot buy two seats for extra room.

## Step 3: Search, Choose, Pay

Search by city, not by station, then read the station names carefully (see the next section). Availability shows as a number of seats; a waitlist symbol means the train is sold out and you are joining China Railway's official queue rather than buying a confirmed seat.

Payment is where foreign visitors hit the second wall: 12306's rails were built around Chinese wallets and UnionPay. Most foreign travellers pay by **Alipay** with a Visa or Mastercard linked inside it — the same setup you use in shops. Card-verify Alipay before you sit down to book, because the seat is held only briefly. If your card is refused, it is almost always the issuer blocking a Chinese merchant, not 12306 rejecting you.

Booking hours are not 24/7: per the [official FAQ](https://www.12306.cn/en/faq.html), sales and changes run **05:00 to 01:00 the next day** (05:00 to 23:30 Tuesdays), while timetable queries and refunds run around the clock.

## Which Station, Exactly? The Mistake That Costs You the Train

Chinese cities have multiple mainline stations and they are not interchangeable. Beijing runs South, West, Chaoyang, Fengtai and the central station; Shanghai has Hongqiao, Shanghai, South and West. Getting this wrong is the most expensive mistake in China rail travel — the stations can be 40 minutes apart and gates close before departure.

- **Beijing South** is the high-speed gateway to Shanghai, Tianjin, Jinan and the east coast. **Beijing West** handles the Guangzhou/Shenzhen corridor and much of the south.
- **Shanghai Hongqiao** is the high-speed hub and sits on the same complex as Hongqiao airport terminal 2 — useful if you are connecting from a domestic flight, useless if your flight lands at Pudong.
- **Zhangjiajie West** serves the national park, three hours from Changsha and about an hour from Fenghuang Ancient Town. Zhangjiajie's airport sits close to the city but has thin schedules, so many visitors fly to Changsha and take the train.
- **Yangshuo station** is roughly 40 minutes' drive from Yangshuo town, even though Guilin North to Yangshuo is only about 30 minutes on the train. Budget the transfer.

## Seat Classes Without the Marketing

Second class is 3+2 seating, comfortable for four to five hours, and the default choice. First class is 2+2 with noticeably more legroom, roughly 1.6× the price. Business class is 1+1 or lie-flat on the newest sets, around 3× second class. On scenic routes — Guilin–Guangzhou, Chengdu–Chongqing — window seats A and F are worth choosing deliberately; sleeper classes on overnight trains are separate products, not seat upgrades, and sell out first.

## At the Station: Security, Gates and the Passport Scan

Every passenger and bag goes through compulsory airport-style screening, and the walk from the entrance to your platform is measured in minutes. Arrive 45–60 minutes early for your first trip from a major hub.

At the gate, scan the passport. Most large stations read foreign passports now, but the readers sit at specific lanes; if the machine throws a red light, don't re-queue there — go to the staffed lane, where a person checks the document against the booking in seconds. That one habit saves more missed trains than any app tip. China Railway halts ticket checking before departure, with the exact cut-off announced locally, so photograph the departure board when you arrive.

## Luggage: What Is Actually Enforced

The free cabin allowance is **20 kg per adult** (10 kg per child, 35 kg for diplomats), each item capped at 20 kg and — on high-speed trains — 130 cm total dimensions. Nobody weighs a backpack, but the dimension rule is enforced visually: a hard-shell case that won't fit the overhead rack goes in the stack at the end of the carriage, and those stacks fill fast. The restricted list also catches people out: nail polish and remover capped at 20 ml, hairspray and aerosols at 120 ml, two lighters. Anything sharper than a small folding knife gets confiscated.

## Changing and Refunding: The Real Fee Tables

Both are allowed once per ticket, and fees are time-based rather than negotiable.

**Changes (改签).** Free more than 48 hours before departure, or 24–48 hours out onto a train leaving before midnight on your original travel date. Free also after departure onto another same-day train. Charged at 5% of the lower fare for changes 24–48 hours out onto a later date, 15% under 24 hours onto a later date, and 40% after departure onto a next-day or later train. Changing your arrival station requires 48 hours' notice and locks out further changes. These percentages come from China Railway's [2024 change-of-ticket announcement](https://www.12306.cn/mormhweb/zxdt/202401/t20240111_40579.html).

**Refunds (退票).** No fee at 8 days or more before departure; 5% between 48 hours and 8 days; 10% between 24 and 48 hours; 20% within 24 hours. After departure, refunds need special station approval and must be handled within two hours; there is no refund once your journey has started, and platform tickets are non-refundable.

Two refinements. Since travel dates from 2 February 2026, registered users booking through official 12306 channels who cancel **within 30 minutes of purchase, at least 4 hours before departure**, get one free refund per order per day — a real safety net for a mistyped date, though it excludes rebooked, waitlisted and group tickets. Refunds also return to the original payment route, which for a foreign card inside Alipay means several working days.

## If the Train Is Sold Out

China Railway runs an official waitlist rather than releasing seats to resellers: you join the queue for a specific train and class, pay up front, and get an automatic refund if it fails. Success rates are good on routes with frequent departures and poor on the last train of a holiday weekend. Fallbacks, in order: shift one station along the corridor (Shanghai Hongqiao to Suzhou, then onward); take an earlier or later departure the same day; or split a long haul at a hub such as Zhengzhou East, Wuhan or Changsha South.

## The Checklist

- Register on 12306 with your passport spelling, exactly as printed.
- Know your release time: the departure station decides it, 15 days out.
- Card-verify Alipay before the window opens.
- Book by city, then confirm the exact station name on the ticket.
- Arrive 45–60 minutes early; go to the staffed lane if the passport reader fails.
- Screenshot the ticket in the app — the itinerary sheet is not a ticket.
- If plans may change, book knowing the 20% same-day refund fee.

# What to Eat in Shanghai: Xiaolongbao, Benbang Cuisine & Street Food

**The short answer**: eat xiaolongbao (soup dumplings) and shengjianbao (pan-fried buns) for breakfast, and book a benbang (本帮) restaurant for dinner — Shanghai's home cooking is sweet, savory, and soy-sauce-rich. Budget ¥120–200 per day for food.

## The Must-Try List

### 1. Xiaolongbao (小笼包) — the soup dumpling
Thin-skinned dumplings filled with pork and hot broth. The ritual: bite a small hole, sip the soup, then eat the dumpling with a touch of vinegar and ginger.

- **Where**: Go where the queue is — it's usually worth it. Din Tai Fung is the polished international version; local shops are cheaper and just as good.
- **Price**: ¥20–40 for a basket of 6–8.

### 2. Shengjianbao (生煎) — pan-fried pork buns
The crispy-bottomed cousin of xiaolongbao — pan-fried until the base is golden and crunchy, with a juicy pork filling. **One liang (两) is 4 pieces — don't over-order.**

- **Where**: **Da Huchun (大壶春)** is the traditional leavened-dough school — a local institution.
- **Price**: ¥10–20 per liang.

### 3. Benbang Cuisine (本帮菜) — Shanghai's home cooking
Sweet, savory, and soy-sauce-heavy: **braised pork belly (红烧肉)**, **sweet-and-sour ribs (糖醋排骨)**, and **scallion oil noodles (葱油拌面)**. Shanghai food is the opposite of spicy — it's comfort food with a sugar kick.

- **Where**: **Lao Jishi (老吉士)** and **Lanxin (兰心)** are local favorites. Book ahead — they're small and popular.
- **Price**: ¥80–150 per person.

### 4. Street Breakfast: Congyou Banmian & More
- **Congyou banmian (葱油拌面)**: Scallion-oil noodles — simple, fragrant, addictive.
- **Xianrou yuebing (鲜肉月饼)**: Savory pork mooncakes, especially popular around Mid-Autumn.
- **Where**: Any busy breakfast shop; Nanjing Road's side streets have plenty.
- **Price**: ¥10–25.

### 5. Autumn Special: Hairy Crab (大闸蟹)
From late September to November, Shanghai goes crab-crazy. Steamed whole, dipped in ginger-vinegar sauce — the roe is the prize.

- **Where**: Any reputable restaurant in season; ask your hotel for a recommendation.
- **Price**: ¥50–150 per crab depending on size.

## Local vs Tourist: Where to Eat

- **Skip**: Restaurants directly on Nanjing Road's main pedestrian strip — tourist-priced.
- **Go**: Side streets off Nanjing Road, the French Concession, and any shop with a queue of locals.
- **Rule of thumb**: If the menu is only in Chinese and the seats are plastic, you're in the right place.

## Shopping Streets (for food & souvenirs)

- **Nanjing Road** is packed with people — the lights are pretty at night, and the classic photo spot is in front of the Peace Hotel (和平饭店).
- For serious shopping, **Nanjing West Road** (Henglong Plaza, Jing'an Kerry Centre) is the luxury zone; **Qipu Road (七浦路)** is the wholesale market where you bargain.

## FAQ

**What food is Shanghai famous for?** Xiaolongbao, shengjianbao, and sweet benbang cuisine.

**Where do locals eat?** Lao Jishi and Lanxin for benbang; queue-worthy xiaolongbao shops everywhere.

**How many shengjianbao should I order?** One liang = 4 pieces. Don't over-order.

**Is Shanghai food spicy?** No — it's sweet and savory, heavy on soy sauce and sugar.

**How much does food cost?** Street food ¥10–30, casual meals ¥50–100, upscale ¥200+.

**What should I eat in autumn?** Hairy crab and xianrou yuebing (savory mooncakes).

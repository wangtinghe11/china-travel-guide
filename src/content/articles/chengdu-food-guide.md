# What to Eat in Chengdu: Hotpot, Chuanchuan & Teahouse Snacks

**The short answer**: eat hotpot with a yuanyang (half-and-half) pot, chuanchuan skewers charged by the stick, and the snack trio of dan dan noodles, Zhong dumplings, and Long Chao Shou. Skip Kuanzhai Alley for food — locals eat at Kuixinglou Street. Budget ¥100–150 per day.

## The Must-Try List

### 1. Hotpot (火锅) — the city's religion
Chengdu hotpot is a bubbling cauldron of chili oil, Sichuan peppercorns, and numbing spice. The ritual: cook your own ingredients, dip in sesame oil, and sweat.

- **Spice warning**: Even "mild" (微辣) can be intense for Western palates. Order a **yuanyang (half-and-half) pot** — half spicy, half clear broth. It's a lifesaver.
- **Where**: Any busy hotpot street. Look for queues of locals.
- **Price**: ¥80–150 per person.

### 2. Chuanchuan (串串) — skewers by the stick
Skewers of meat, vegetables, and tofu cooked in a bubbling pot, **charged by the stick** — usually ¥50–80 per person. Cheaper and more social than a full hotpot.

- **Where**: Chuanchuan shops across the city; Kuixinglou Street has plenty.
- **Price**: ¥50–80 per person.

### 3. The Snack Trio
- **Dan dan noodles (担担面)**: Spicy sesame-peanut noodles — the classic.
- **Zhong dumplings (钟水饺)**: Small pork dumplings in a sweet-spicy chili sauce.
- **Long Chao Shou (龙抄手)**: Chengdu's wontons, often in a red-oil broth.
- **Where**: Any busy snack shop; the famous brand-name shops are touristy but still good.
- **Price**: ¥10–20 each.

### 4. Rabbit Head (兔头) — the brave snack
A Chengdu specialty: spicy, savory, and surprisingly meaty. You pick the meat off the skull — it's a local obsession.

- **Where**: Specialty shops; ask your hotel for a recommendation.
- **Price**: ¥15–25 per head.

### 5. Teahouse Snacks
Chengdu's teahouse culture is everywhere — find any **坝坝茶 (open-air tea stall)** and sit for an afternoon. Order a gaiwan of jasmine tea with peanuts and sunflower seeds.

- **Where**: People's Park (Heming Teahouse) is the classic spot.
- **Price**: Tea ¥20–40, snacks ¥10–20.

## Local vs Tourist: Where to Eat

- **Skip**: Kuanzhai Alley (宽窄巷子) — worth a stroll for the atmosphere, but the food is tourist-priced.
- **Go**: **Kuixinglou Street (奎星楼街)** — that's where locals actually eat. Any busy small shop with a queue is a safe bet.
- **Rule of thumb**: If the menu is only in Chinese and the seats are plastic, you're in the right place.

## Local Phrases

Learn **"巴适" (bashi, comfortable/great)** and **"要得" (yaode, OK/sure)** — locals light up when foreigners use them. Say "bashi" after a good meal and you'll make friends.

## FAQ

**What food is Chengdu famous for?** Hotpot, chuanchuan, dan dan noodles, Zhong dumplings, Long Chao Shou, and rabbit head.

**Is hotpot too spicy?** Even "mild" can be intense — order a yuanyang pot.

**Is chuanchuan cheaper?** Yes — ¥50–80 per person, charged by the stick.

**Where do locals eat?** Kuixinglou Street, not Kuanzhai Alley.

**What's the snack trio?** Dan dan noodles, Zhong dumplings, Long Chao Shou.

**How much does food cost?** Street food ¥10–30, chuanchuan ¥50–80, hotpot ¥80–150 per person.

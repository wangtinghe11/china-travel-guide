# Guangzhou Travel Guide 2026: Itineraries, Transport & Insider Tips

Guangzhou is the heart of Cantonese culture — the birthplace of dim sum, a city of colonial architecture and neon skylines, and the gateway to southern China. Here's the complete guide.

## Day 1: Old Canton

**Morning**: **Dim sum breakfast (yum cha)** — the Cantonese ritual of small dishes and tea. Try **Dian Dou De (点都德)** or **Tao Tao Ju (陶陶居)**, about ¥60–100 per person. Order a few classics: har gow (shrimp dumplings), siu mai, and char siu bao.

**Afternoon**: **Shamian Island (沙面)** — a quiet, leafy enclave of European colonial buildings. Perfect for a slow walk and photos, and far less crowded than the tourist core.

**Evening**: **Canton Tower (广州塔)** — don't go up; the tower is best seen from **Huacheng Square (花城广场)** across the river, especially at night when it lights up. The **Pearl River night cruise (珠江夜游)** is a nice alternative — ¥80–150 for an hour, departing from Tianzi Wharf (天字码头) or Dashatou (大沙头).

## Day 2: Food & Culture

**Morning**: **Chen Clan Ancestral Hall (陈家祠)** — the finest example of Lingnan architecture in the city, ¥10 entry. Carvings, ceramics, and courtyards.

**Afternoon**: **Beijing Road (北京路)** shopping street — and the old **Xiguan (西关)** neighborhood for traditional Cantonese houses. Try **Yinji rice rolls (银记肠粉)**, **Nanxin double-skin milk (南信双皮奶)**, and **Baohua noodles (宝华面店)** — all local institutions. For dessert, **sweet soups (糖水)** like double-skin milk and mango pomelo sago are a must — **Nanxin (南信)** and **Baihua (百花)** are the old favorites, still queuing at 10 PM.

**Evening**: **Char siu (烧腊)** from a roadside shop — "cut meat" (斩料) with rice is the perfect Cantonese meal. Cheap, fast, and delicious.

## Day 3 (Optional): Chimelong or Foshan

**Chimelong** (长隆) is one of Asia's best theme parks — direct on Metro Line 3, great for families. Or take a short train to **Foshan** (30 minutes by high-speed rail) for the ancestral temple and martial-arts heritage — the **Ancestral Temple (祖庙)** and **Lingnan Tiandi (岭南天地)** are the highlights. Zhuhai and Shenzhen are about 1 hour away.

## Where to Eat Like a Local

- **Dim sum**: Dian Dou De and Tao Tao Ju are the big names — go early (before 10 AM) to avoid the queue.
- **Rice rolls (肠粉)**: Yinji is the classic — silky, savory, perfect for breakfast.
- **Double-skin milk (双皮奶)**: Nanxin's version is legendary — a custard-like dessert. Mango pomelo sago (杨枝甘露) is the other must-try sweet soup.
- **Char siu (烧腊)**: Any busy roadside shop. Point at what looks good, get it with rice.
- **Herbal tea (凉茶)**: Huang Zhenlong (黄振龙) or Wang Laoji (王老吉) — bitter but effective for the heat. A true Guangzhou experience.

## Practical Tips

- **Weather**: Summer is brutally hot and humid (feels like 40°C) with sudden rainstorms — carry an umbrella. Winter is mild — 15–20°C — a great escape from the north.
- **Metro**: Excellent, cheap, English-signed. Line 3 goes to Chimelong.
- **Language**: Cantonese is the local language, but Mandarin works everywhere. Learn "m goi" (唔该, thank you) — it goes a long way.
- **Money**: Alipay and WeChat Pay are universal; carry a little cash as backup.
- **Wholesale markets**: Shisanhang (十三行) and Baima (白马) are the famous wholesale markets — go in the morning, they close in the afternoon.

## Where to Stay

- **Zhujiang New Town (珠江新城)** — modern, near the tower
- **Beijing Road** — central, shopping and food
- **Shamian Island** — quiet, colonial charm

# Is China Safe for Tourists? 2026 Safety Guide

China is one of the safest countries for tourists. Violent crime against foreigners is extremely rare, and even petty theft is less common than in most Western cities. The biggest risks are scams, traffic, and food safety — all easily avoidable with basic precautions.

## Safety in China: The Big Picture

- **Excellent public safety** — China has very low violent crime rates, and walking alone late at night is generally safe
- **Emergency numbers (national)**: Police **110**, Ambulance **120**, Fire **119**
- **Pickpocketing** happens in crowded tourist spots — keep your wallet and phone in front pockets
- **Ignore "black car" drivers** who approach you at train stations and scenic area entrances — use Didi instead
- **Use metered taxis only** — refuse any taxi that won't turn on the meter

## Common Scams to Avoid

- **The "free" invitation** — tea houses, calligraphy shows, and massage parlors that invite you in "for free" end with a huge bill. Decline any free invitation, especially for tea, massage, or calligraphy performances.
- **Taxi route inflation** — some drivers take long routes to inflate the fare. Use Didi or 高德 (Amap) for transparent pricing.
- **"Helpful" locals** — at scenic areas, strangers offering to guide you often lead you to overpriced shops.
- **Souvenir overcharging** — bargain hard, aim for around 30% of the asking price. If they won't negotiate, walk away.
- **Currency exchange** — only use banks or ATMs. Never trust street-side "high rate" money changers.

## Transport Safety

- **Crossing streets** — cars yield to pedestrians in China, but **e-bikes and scooters do not**. Look both ways, always.
- **E-bikes everywhere** — don't walk with headphones in; e-bikes are quiet and fast.
- **High-speed rail & metro** — security checks are strict but the system is extremely safe. Ride with confidence.
- **Seatbelts** — wear them in taxis and ride-hailing cars (rear seats too, if available).

## Food & Health

- **Street food** is generally safe, but if you have a sensitive stomach, choose proper restaurants.
- **Drink bottled or boiled water** — tap water is not potable in China.
- **Pharmacies are everywhere** — major chains like 老百姓 (Laobaixing) and 益丰 (Yifeng) stock common medicines.
- **Hospitals** — big hospitals have international departments, and costs are far lower than in Europe or the US.

## Practical Tips

- **Emergency contacts** — save 110/120/119 in your phone, and keep your hotel front desk number handy. Tell a Chinese friend where you're going.
- **Travel insurance** — buy a policy that includes medical coverage. Chinese hospitals charge foreigners fairly, but insurance gives peace of mind.

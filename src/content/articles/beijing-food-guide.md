# What to Eat in Beijing: Peking Duck, Street Food & Local Favorites

**The short answer**: eat Peking duck at Siji Minfu or Da Dong, grab a jianbing for breakfast, and work through the classics at Huguosi Snack Street. Skip douzhi unless you're brave. Budget ¥100–150 per day for food.

## The Must-Try List

### 1. Peking Duck (北京烤鸭) — the city's signature
Crispy lacquered skin carved tableside, wrapped in thin pancakes with scallions, cucumber, and sweet bean sauce. The skin is the star — dip it in sugar first, then wrap the rest.

- **Where locals go**: **Siji Minfu (四季民福)** and **Da Dong (大董)**. Book ahead — the queues are real.
- **Where tourists go**: Quanjude (全聚德) — historic and famous, but locals rarely queue for it.
- **Price**: ¥150–300 per person at a good restaurant.

### 2. Zhajiangmian (炸酱面) — the everyday classic
Wheat noodles tossed with fermented soybean paste, minced pork, and fresh vegetable shreds (cucumber, radish, bean sprouts). Beijing's comfort food — every family has their own recipe.

- **Where**: Any busy noodle shop; hutong restaurants do it well.
- **Price**: ¥20–40.

### 3. Breakfast Classics: Chaogan & Luzhu
- **Chaogan (炒肝)**: A thick, garlicky stew of pork liver and intestines — savory, hearty, and surprisingly mild.
- **Luzhu (卤煮)**: Braised pork offal (lung, intestine, tofu) in a rich broth with bread chunks. An acquired taste, but a Beijing institution.
- **Where**: Traditional breakfast shops; **Huguosi Snack Street (护国寺小吃)** has the most complete selection of classics.
- **Price**: ¥15–30.

### 4. Jianbing (煎饼) — the street breakfast
A savory crepe of egg, crispy cracker, scallions, and chili sauce, folded hot off the griddle. The ultimate grab-and-go breakfast.

- **Where**: Any street stall with a queue before 9 AM.
- **Price**: ¥8–15.

### 5. Hutong Snacks & Sweets
- **Lüda gun (驴打滚)**: Glutinous rice rolls coated in soybean flour.
- **Wandouhuang (豌豆黄)**: Chilled pea-flour cake — light and sweet.
- **Tanghulu (糖葫芦)**: Candied hawthorn skewers, a winter street staple.
- **Where**: Huguosi Snack Street, or any hutong snack shop.

## Local vs Tourist: Where to Eat

- **Skip**: Wangfujing's food stalls and restaurants — it's a tourist street, overpriced and tuned for one-time visitors.
- **Go**: Xidan, hutong restaurants around Wudaoying and Beiluoguxiang, and any shop with a queue of locals.
- **Rule of thumb**: If the menu is only in Chinese and the seats are plastic, you're in the right place.

## Shopping Streets (for food souvenirs)

- **Wangfujing** is the tourist strip — fine for a stroll, but locals shop at **Xidan** and **SKP**.
- **Xiushui Street (秀水街)** is the bargaining market — haggle down to about 30% of the asking price (3 折).

## FAQ

**Where do locals eat Peking duck?** Siji Minfu and Da Dong. Quanjude is the tourist spot.

**Should I try douzhi?** Only if you're brave — it's fermented and sour. Chaogan and luzhu are safer bets.

**What's the best breakfast?** Jianbing from a street stall, or chaogan/luzhu at a traditional shop. Huguosi has the widest selection.

**Where to try Beijing snacks?** Huguosi Snack Street. For shopping, Xidan and SKP over Wangfujing.

**How much does food cost?** Street food ¥10–30, casual meals ¥40–80, Peking duck ¥150–300 per person.

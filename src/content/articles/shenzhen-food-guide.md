# What to Eat in Shenzhen: Beef Hotpot, Coconut Chicken & All-China Cuisine

**The short answer**: eat Chaoshan beef hotpot (潮汕牛肉火锅) at Baheli (八合里), try coconut chicken (椰子鸡) — a Shenzhen invention — and explore the immigrant city's all-China food scene. Budget ¥80–120 for hotpot, ¥60–80 for coconut chicken.

## The Must-Try List

### 1. Chaoshan Beef Hotpot (潮汕牛肉火锅) — the city's signature
Paper-thin slices of fresh beef cooked for seconds in a clear broth, then dipped in a savory sauce of soy, garlic, and chili. The beef is cut to order — different cuts for different cooking times. **Baheli (八合里)** is the famous chain: order the **fatty brisket (肥牛)** and **hand-cut beef balls (手打牛肉丸)**.

- **Where**: Baheli (八合里) — multiple branches across the city.
- **Price**: ¥80–120 per person.
- **Tip**: The broth is clear and light — the beef is the star. Don't drown it in sauce.

### 2. Coconut Chicken (椰子鸡) — a Shenzhen invention
Chicken simmered in fresh coconut water with coconut meat, served with a dipping sauce of lime, chili, and soy. Light, refreshing, and unique to Shenzhen. The broth is sweet and fragrant — drink it as soup at the end.

- **Where**: Any busy coconut chicken restaurant — they're everywhere.
- **Price**: ¥60–80 per person.

### 3. Seafood (海鲜) — the coastal advantage
Shenzhen's coastal villages have excellent fresh seafood markets. Pick your own from the tanks and have it cooked to order.

- **Where**: Coastal areas like Shekou (蛇口) and the eastern beaches.
- **Price**: ¥80–150 per person depending on what you pick.

### 4. All-China Cuisine — the immigrant city's gift
Shenzhen was founded in 1980, and its people came from everywhere — so every regional cuisine in China is here. Sichuan, Hunan, Cantonese, Chaoshan, Xinjiang, Dongbei... you name it. This is the only city in China where you can eat your way across the whole country in one week.

- **Where**: Any mall food court or restaurant street.
- **Price**: ¥40–100 per person.

### 5. OCT Loft (华侨城创意园) — cafés & creative food
A converted factory complex with art galleries, design shops, and great cafés. On weekends there's a creative market with food stalls and handmade goods.

- **Where**: OCT Loft, Nanshan district.
- **Price**: ¥30–80.

## Local vs Tourist: Where to Eat

- **Skip**: The food courts at Window of the World and other tourist attractions.
- **Go**: Baheli for beef hotpot, any busy coconut chicken place, and the seafood villages on the coast.
- **Shopping food**: Dongmen (东门) and Huaqiangbei have cheap street food — perfect for a quick bite between electronics browsing.

## Street Food vs Sit-Down

- **Street food (¥10–40)**: Skewers, grilled seafood, and the famous **Chaoshan beef balls** on sticks.
- **Sit-down (¥60–120)**: Beef hotpot and coconut chicken — the two signature experiences.

## Practical Tips

- **Pace of life**: Shenzheners walk fast and work hard — restaurants are efficient, and queues move quickly.
- **No tipping**: Tipping is not expected.
- **Payment**: Alipay and WeChat Pay are universal — Shenzhen is one of China's most cashless cities.

## FAQ

**What food is Shenzhen famous for?** Chaoshan beef hotpot and coconut chicken — plus every regional cuisine in China, thanks to the immigrant population.

**What is Chaoshan beef hotpot?** Paper-thin beef cooked for seconds in clear broth, dipped in savory sauce. Baheli is the famous chain.

**What is coconut chicken?** Chicken simmered in fresh coconut water with lime-chili-soy dipping sauce — a Shenzhen invention.

**Is Shenzhen food spicy?** Not by default — the local specialties are light and fresh. But you can find every regional cuisine, including spicy Sichuan and Hunan.

**Where should I eat?** Baheli for beef hotpot, any busy coconut chicken restaurant, and the coastal seafood villages.

Chengdu is the home of giant pandas, fiery Sichuan cuisine, and a famously relaxed teahouse culture.

## Highlights

- **Chengdu Panda Base** — see pandas up close, go early
- **Wenshu Monastery** — peaceful temple and vegetarian food
- **People's Park** — teahouses, ear cleaning, and mahjong
- **Jinli & Kuanzhai Alleys** — traditional streets
- **Leshan Giant Buddha** — day trip, 71 m cliff carving

## Getting Around

The metro is modern and English-signed. The panda base is a 30-minute Didi ride from the center. Buses and bikes cover the rest.

## Where to Stay

- **Chunxi Road** — shopping and dining
- **Kuanzhai Alleys** — traditional charm
- **Tianfu Square** — central

## Food

Hotpot (spicy!), mapo tofu, dan dan noodles, and chuanchuan (skewers). Sichuan food is famously numbing-spicy — start mild.

## Local Insider Tips

- **Panda Base**: be at the gate for the 7:30 opening. Pandas eat and play in the cool morning and are flat asleep by 9:00 — a 10 a.m. arrival is a wasted ticket.
- **Hotpot heat**: when a local says "mild" (微辣) it is still seriously hot for a first-timer. Order a **split pot (鸳鸯锅)** — half mild, half full — and you keep the option open.
- **Kuanzhai Alleys are for strolling, not eating**: the food there is tourist-priced. Eat on **Kuixinglou Street (奎星楼街)**, one metro stop away, where locals queue.
- **Heming Teahouse** in People's Park (人民公园鹤鸣茶社) is the real Chengdu: a covered-bowl tea, a bamboo chair, and the ear-cleaning guys who come round with tuning forks. Budget an afternoon, not an hour.
- **Chuanchuan (串串)** is charged per skewer — expect ¥50–80 a head, cheaper than hotpot. Rabbit head (兔头) is the local test of nerve; it's mostly about the chilli oil.
- **Dujiangyan** is 30 minutes by high-speed rail and is a 2,000-year-old irrigation system still working — more impressive in person than the description sounds.
- **Jiuyanqiao (九眼桥)** is the bar strip at night; loud, young, and right on the river.
- **The snack trio**: dan dan noodles, Zhong dumplings (钟水饺, sweetened chilli oil) and Long chaoshou (龙抄手, wontons). Eat them at a neighbourhood shop, not a "snack museum".
- **Learn two words**: "巴适" (bāshì, comfortable/great) and "要得" (yàodé, works for me). Locals light up.

## Practical Details

- **Two airports, and they are not interchangeable**: Tianfu (TFU) is far east — about an hour by Metro Line 18. Shuangliu (CTU) is close to the city. Check your ticket before you leave the hotel.
- **Metro**: scan with Alipay or WeChat. To the Panda Base, take Line 3 to the Panda Avenue stop and transfer to the shuttle.
- **Weather**: overcast and damp most of the year — carry an umbrella every day, not just in "rainy season".
- **Time needed**: 2 days for pandas plus the old city; 3–4 days to add Dujiangyan and Leshan's Giant Buddha. Jiuzhaigou and Daocheng are beautiful but too far for a short trip.

Shenzhen, once a fishing village, is now China's tech capital — a futuristic city of glass towers, innovation, and theme parks.

## Highlights

- **Ping An Finance Centre** — 599 m, one of the world's tallest
- **OCT Loft** — creative arts district
- **Window of the World** — mini replicas of world landmarks
- **Dameisha Beach** — escape the city
- **Hong Kong border** — 30 minutes by metro

## Getting Around

The metro is modern, cheap, and English-signed. Shenzhen is the easiest city to enter from Hong Kong — take the MTR East Rail to Lo Wu or Lok Ma Chau, then connect to the metro.

## Where to Stay

- **Futian** — central, business district
- **Luohu** — near the Hong Kong border
- **Nanshan** — tech hub, coastal walks

## Food

Cantonese seafood, hotpot chains, and street food. Shenzhen is a food melting pot — you'll find every regional Chinese cuisine here.

## Local Insider Tips

- **There is no old town.** Shenzhen was a fishing village until 1980 — the appeal is glass towers, parks and the border, so plan for modern China rather than heritage China.
- **Huaqiangbei (华强北)** is the electronics market the whole world copies. Bargain hard — start at 50% — and don't buy anything expensive or "branded" here.
- **Shenzhen Bay Park**: rent a bike and ride the coastal path at sunset. You are looking across the water at Hong Kong's New Territories the whole way.
- **Window of the World** (世界之窗) is ¥220 and is essentially a photo park of scale models — fine with kids, skippable without them.
- **Chaoshan beef hotpot** at Baheli (八合里) is the local obsession: the beef is butchered to order and the broth is clear, not spicy.
- **Coconut chicken** (椰子鸡) is Shenzhen's own dish — Wenchang chicken poached in young coconut water, dipped in soy with lime. Light, sweet, nothing like Cantonese roast.
- **To Hong Kong**: Futian and Luohu checkpoints put you in Hong Kong in about 30 minutes — but mainland citizens need a permit, and foreign passport holders need to check their visa/entry terms before crossing.
- **OCT Loft (华侨城创意园)** — converted factory buildings, weekend design markets, decent coffee. The closest thing to a cultural quarter.
- **Talent Park (人才公园)** in Nanshan has a free evening light show over Shenzhen Bay with the bridge as backdrop.
- **Shopping tiers**: MixC and One Avenue for luxury; Dongmen and Huaqiangbei for cheap. Electronics are genuinely the cheapest in China here.

## Practical Details

- **Airport**: Bao'an (SZX) looks like a space station inside; Metro Line 11 gets you to Futian in about 30 minutes.
- **Weather**: subtropical — summer runs April to November. Typhoon season is July to September: watch the forecasts, flights get cancelled.
- **Pace**: this is a city that works hard. People walk fast and the metro is packed at 9 a.m. and 6 p.m., but the parks and greenery are outstanding.
- **Time needed**: 1–2 days is enough; most visitors use it as a Hong Kong or Guangzhou stopover.

Xi'an, the ancient capital of 13 dynasties, is home to the Terracotta Army and the best-preserved city wall in China.

## Highlights

- **Terracotta Army** — 8,000 life-size warriors, 1 hour from the city
- **City Wall** — cycle the 14 km perimeter at sunset
- **Muslim Quarter** — street food paradise
- **Big Wild Goose Pagoda** — Tang dynasty landmark
- **Shaanxi History Museum** — free but book ahead

## Getting Around

The metro covers the main sights. The Terracotta Army is easiest by tourist bus 5 (306) or a Didi ride (~¥150). City wall bikes rent at the gates.

## Where to Stay

- **Bell Tower area** — central, walkable
- **Muslim Quarter** — food at your doorstep
- **Qujiang** — modern, near the pagoda

## Food

Biangbiang noodles, roujiamo (Chinese burger), and yangrou paomo (lamb soup with bread). The Muslim Quarter is the place to graze.

## Local Insider Tips

- **Terracotta Army**: book on the official site or WeChat mini-program in advance — there is no useful on-site ticket window. Hire a licensed guide at the entrance (¥100–200); without one the pits are just rows of clay figures with no context.
- **City Wall**: rent a bike at any gate and ride the full 13.7 km loop. Late afternoon is the best light, and the south gate (Yongningmen) is the busiest — the west gate is quieter.
- **Muslim Quarter is a tourist street**: locals eat in **Sajinqiao (洒金桥)** and **Dapiyuan (大皮院)**, five minutes' walk away. Same food, half the price, no queue.
- **Shaanxi History Museum** is free but the online slot allocation is brutal — release is 7 days ahead and it sells out in minutes. **Xi'an Museum** (also free, inside the Small Wild Goose Pagoda park) is a genuinely good substitute.
- **Datang Everbright City** (大唐不夜城) is free and at its best after dark, but the light show draws enormous crowds. Walk the far (south) end first and drift north as it empties.
- **Yongxingfang (永兴坊)** is where the "bowl-smashing wine" gimmick started — cleaner and calmer than the Muslim Quarter, and locals do go.
- **Shuyuanmen (书院门)** is the calligraphy and brush street by the south gate: quieter than the Muslim Quarter, and you can watch ink being ground and seals being carved.
- **Beilin Museum (碑林)** is the stone-forest of classical calligraphy — ¥65, and worth it if you care about writing. Yan Zhenqing and Liu Gongquan originals are here.
- **Noodles**: biangbiang, oil-splashed (油泼面) and belt noodles are better in any random neighbourhood shop than in a scenic-area restaurant. Order a **Bingfeng** orange soda with them like a local.
- **Hanfu photos**: ¥100–300 for a full outfit plus styling; the city wall and the Big Wild Goose Pagoda are the classic backdrops.
- **Skip if short on time**: Tang Paradise (大唐芙蓉园, ¥120) is a reconstructed garden — pretty, but pricey for what it is.
- **Huashan**: the plank walk (长空栈道) is the draw, but take the cable car if your fitness isn't there. It is a full day from the city.

## Practical Details

- **Airport**: Xianyang (XIY) is 40 km out. Metro Line 14 now reaches it; the airport bus is ¥25 and takes about an hour.
- **Taxis** start at ¥8.5 — cheap — but the ring road jams at rush hour. Metro beats taxis for anything inside the walls.
- **Weather**: dry. Summer hits 38°C+, and spring can bring dust storms (sand from the north). Carry water and lip balm.
- **Time needed**: 2 days covers the warriors, the wall and the night city; 3 days adds Huashan or a proper museum day.

Guangzhou, the capital of Guangdong, is southern China's trading powerhouse and the home of Cantonese cuisine.

## Highlights

- **Canton Tower** — 600 m observation tower, best at night
- **Shamian Island** — colonial architecture, peaceful walks
- **Chen Clan Ancestral Hall** — exquisite folk art
- **Yuexiu Park** — the Five Rams statue
- **Pearl River Night Cruise** — skyline views

## Getting Around

The metro is extensive and English-signed. Baiyun Airport connects to the city by metro Line 3. Guangzhou is a major hub for visa-free transit travelers.

## Where to Stay

- **Zhujiang New Town** — modern, near Canton Tower
- **Beijing Road** — shopping and street food
- **Shamian Island** — quiet, historic

## Food

Dim sum (morning tea), roast goose, and wonton noodles. Guangzhou is the best city in China for Cantonese food — go where the locals queue.

## Local Insider Tips

- **Morning tea (早茶)** is the main event: ¥60–100 a head at **Diandude (点都德)** or **Taotaoju (陶陶居)**. Go before 9 a.m. on a weekday — weekends mean a queue ticket and a 40-minute wait.
- **Don't pay to climb Canton Tower.** The best photo of it is from **Huacheng Square (花城广场)** at night, free, from ground level.
- **Shamian Island** (沙面) — the old colonial concession — is quiet, tree-lined and full of wedding photographers. The best place in the city to walk with no agenda.
- **Chen Clan Ancestral Hall** (陈家祠) is the high point of Lingnan architecture — the roof ridge is a full opera in ceramic — and it costs ¥10.
- **Old-school noodles**: Yinji rice rolls (银记肠粉), Nanxin double-skin milk (南信双皮奶, also does mango sago), Baohua noodle shop. Order the small portions and eat at three places.
- **Roast meat counters** (烧腊) are the real everyday meal: point at a hanging goose or char siu, and they chop it over rice for ¥25–35.
- **Dim sum desserts** — double-skin milk, mango pomelo sago — at Nanxin or Baihua; both still have a queue at 10 p.m.
- **Chimelong** (长隆) is the reason families come; Metro Line 3 goes straight there. Allow a full day per park.
- **Wholesale markets**: Shisanhang (十三行) and Baima (白马) for clothing open early and shut mid-afternoon. Morning only.
- **Herbal tea** (凉茶): Wong Lo Kat or Huang Zhenlong. Bitter, and genuinely useful in this humidity.
- **Learn one word**: "唔该" (m̀h gōi — thank you / excuse me). It buys you a lot of goodwill in Cantonese shops.

## Practical Details

- **Weather**: humid enough that 30°C feels like 40°C, with sudden downpours. Winter (Dec–Feb) is 15–20°C — this is where southern China goes to escape the cold.
- **Rail**: 30 minutes to Foshan (ancestral temple, Lingnan Tiandi), 1 hour to Zhuhai and Shenzhen. Guangzhou is the best rail hub in the south.
- **Time needed**: 2 days for the city and morning tea; add Chimelong or Foshan for a third.

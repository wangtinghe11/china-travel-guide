Beijing is China's political and cultural heart — a city of imperial palaces, ancient hutongs, and futuristic architecture. Most first-time visitors to China start here.

## Highlights

- **Forbidden City** — the world's largest palace complex, book tickets online in advance
- **Great Wall** — Mutianyu (restored, cable car) or Jinshanling (wild, hiking)
- **Temple of Heaven** — morning tai chi in the park
- **Hutongs** — old alleyways around Shichahai and Nanluoguxiang
- **Summer Palace** — imperial gardens by the lake

## Local Insider Tips

- **Forbidden City tickets**: Must be booked **7 days ahead** on the official website or WeChat mini-program. No on-site sales, and it's closed on Mondays.
- **Great Wall**: Go to **Mutianyu** — fewer crowds, beautiful scenery. Badaling is packed with tour groups.
- **Peking duck**: Locals eat at **Siji Minfu (四季民福)** or **Da Dong (大董)**. Quanjude is a tourist trap.
- **Hutongs**: Try **Wudaoying (五道营)** or **Beiluoguxiang (北锣鼓巷)**. Nanluoguxiang is now fully commercialized.
- **Airports**: Daxing is far from the city; Capital Airport is closer. The airport express train takes 25 minutes.
- **Tiananmen**: Expect long security lines. For the flag-raising ceremony, arrive by 4 AM to get a spot.
- **Jingshan Park**: ¥2 entry, best panoramic view of the Forbidden City — especially at sunset.
- **Metro**: Scan with Alipay. Rush hour is packed — avoid 7–9 AM and 5–7 PM if you can.
- **Food**: Don't try douzhi (fermented mung bean drink) unless you're brave — it's sour and funky. Chaogan (liver stew) and luzhu (braised pork) are safer bets.
- **Best time to visit**: April–May (flowers) and September–October (crisp autumn air).

## Getting Around

The Beijing metro is cheap, fast, and English-signed. Buy a transit card or use Alipay's transport QR code. Taxis are metered; Didi (Chinese Uber) is the most reliable way to hail one.

## Where to Stay

- **Wangfujing / Dongcheng** — central, near Forbidden City
- **Sanlitun** — nightlife and dining
- **Qianmen** — traditional atmosphere near Tiananmen

## Food

Peking duck (Siji Minfu or Da Dong), zhajiangmian (noodles with soybean paste), and jianbing (savory crepes) from street stalls. Most restaurants accept Alipay/WeChat Pay; carry some cash as backup.
